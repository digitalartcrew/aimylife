The Gap

"I can use AI" is not an offer

Nobody buys a skill. They buy a specific job, done, by a specific date, for a known price.

What people say

"I can help you with AI." "I do AI automation." "I'm an AI consultant."

All true. None of them is something a busy owner can say yes to.

What gets bought

"I'll write replies to your last 200 Google reviews, in your voice, in five days, for $400."

A job, a timeframe, a price. Yes or no.

Session 1 • Finding the Offer

Where your offer actually lives

Fill in three columns. The offer is where all three overlap — not where any one of them is strongest.

What you already know

The industry you worked in. The software you know. The people who already trust you.

What they lose sleep over

Not "efficiency." The specific recurring thing: the inbox, the reviews, the quotes, the listings.

What has a budget line

Someone is already paying for marketing, admin, or a VA. Easier to redirect a budget than create one.

Steal One of These

Five services local businesses buy

  • 1 Review replies. Every unanswered review is a visible gap. Priced per batch.
  • 2 Listing and product descriptions. Tedious, high volume, immediately visible result.
  • 3 Inbox triage setup. Reply templates and a sorting system, handed over with a loom video.
  • 4 The monthly newsletter. Recurring revenue, and most owners genuinely dread writing it.
  • 5 SOP writing. Turn how-we-do-it-here into written procedures for onboarding staff.
Pricing

Never bill by the hour for this

AI makes the work fast. Hourly billing turns that speed into a pay cut. Price the finished thing.

The hourly trap

You quote four hours. AI does it in forty minutes. You either overcharge for time you did not spend, or you earn a quarter of what the work was worth. There is no honest version.

Price the deliverable

"200 review replies, five days, $400." Nobody asks how long it took. Your speed becomes your margin instead of your penalty.

Rule of thumb for a first offer: name a price that makes you slightly uncomfortable, then do not discount it when they pause. The pause is normal.
Structure

Always give three options

One price is a yes-or-no question. Three prices is a which-one question.

Starter

The job, done once. Low risk, low price. This is how a stranger becomes a client.

Standard

The job plus the system, so they can repeat it. This is the one you want them to pick.

Ongoing

Monthly. Recurring revenue is the whole point — one of these is worth ten one-off jobs.

Session 2 • Paperwork

The ten-minute proposal

Write a one-page proposal. Plain language, no consultant-speak. Client: [who they are, in their words] Problem they described: [quote them directly] What I'll deliver: [the specific thing] By when: [date] Three options: [starter / standard / ongoing, with prices] What I need from them: [access, logins, approvals] Open by repeating their problem back to them in one sentence. End with one clear next step.
The opening sentence does most of the work. People buy from whoever proves they were listening.
Protect Yourself

Four clauses, every time

1. Scope, in nouns

"200 replies" not "review support." Write what is not included too. That line prevents most disputes.

2. Revisions, with a number

"Two rounds included, then $X per round." Unlimited revisions is how a $400 job becomes a $40 job.

3. Payment terms

Deposit up front, balance on delivery. Say what happens if they pay late, before they pay late.

4. AI disclosure

Say plainly that AI tools are used and that you review the output. Some clients require it; all of them respect it.

This is not legal advice. For anything substantial, have a real template reviewed by a lawyer in your jurisdiction.
Getting Paid

The part most people get wrong

  • 1 Deposit before you start. 50% up front. It is not rude, it is standard, and it filters out the clients who were never going to pay.
  • 2 Invoice the same day you deliver. Not Friday. Not "when I get a chance." The same day.
  • 3 Automate the chase. Any invoicing tool will send the reminders for you. Let it be the one that nags.
  • 4 Set money aside for tax. From the first payment, not from the first tax bill.
Straight Talk

What AI will not do for you

Find the clients

The first three come from people who already know you. That part is conversations, not prompts.

Build the trust

Showing up, doing what you said, on the day you said. No tool shortcuts this.

Make it quick

First paid job in a month is a good outcome. Anyone promising faster is selling you something.

Recap & Hands-On Lab

What to do before next time

The four takeaways

  • Price the deliverable, never the hour
  • Three options, not one
  • Deposit up front, invoice same day
