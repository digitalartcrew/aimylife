Diagnose First

Find the repetitive, text-shaped work

Not everything should be automated. The jobs worth handing over share three traits — and in most small businesses they are all sitting in the same two places.

It repeats

You have answered this question, in these words, more than ten times.

It is low-judgement

Getting it wrong is annoying, not expensive. A draft you review is good enough.

It is text

An email, a caption, a description, a quote. Text in, text out.

Session 1 • Foundation

Build the source of truth first

One document. Everything the AI needs to sound like your business instead of any business.

What goes in it

- What we do, in one sentence, in our words - Hours, location, service area, how to reach us - Prices, or the honest reason we quote instead - Our 15 most-asked questions, with the answers we actually give - Three things we always say, and three we never say - What we do when something goes wrong
Paste this at the top of any chat before you ask for customer-facing text. Everything downstream gets better, and you only write it once.
Session 1 • The Front Desk

Three pieces, in this order

1. Drafted replies

You still press send. The AI gets you from blank to 90% so the inbox stops being a wall.

2. The self-serve FAQ

The top fifteen questions, answered on your site. The best support ticket is the one never opened.

3. A booking link

Kill "what times work for you?" entirely. This one is a calendar tool, not an AI problem.

Inbox Workflow

Draft, review, send

You are writing as [business name]. Here is our source-of-truth doc: [paste] Draft a reply to the customer email below. Rules: - Under 120 words - Answer the actual question in the first sentence - One clear next step at the end - Plain words. No "we appreciate your reaching out" - If the answer is not in our doc, say what you need from me instead of guessing Email: [paste]
That last rule is the important one. Without it you will eventually send a customer a confident, invented answer.
Session 2 • Marketing

Write the brand brief once

Every piece of content this month starts from this. It takes twenty minutes to write.

Who we serve: [the specific person, not "everyone"] What they worry about: [in their words, not yours] What we sell: [the outcome, not the service] Why us and not them: [one honest sentence] Voice: [three adjectives + one example sentence] Never say: [jargon, claims, phrases you hate] Proof we can use: [reviews, numbers, before-and-afters]
Prompt Chaining

One brief, four steps, a month of content

  • 1 Pillars. "From this brief, give me four content themes we could credibly talk about all month."
  • 2 Posts. "For each theme, write three short posts. Vary the format: one tip, one story, one question."
  • 3 Newsletter. "Turn the strongest theme into a 300-word email with one call to action."
  • 4 Flyer. "Pull the headline, three bullets, and the call to action for a one-page printed flyer."
Each step reads the step before it. That is the whole trick — do not start a fresh chat in the middle.
Spending Decisions

Four questions before you pay for anything

Does it save two hours a month?

If not, it is a toy. Two hours a month is roughly the floor where a subscription pays for itself.

Can you leave?

Can you export your data and your templates on the way out? If not, you are renting your own business.

What does it touch?

A tool with access to your inbox and customer list is a different risk from one that writes captions.

Who fixes it at 7am?

If it breaks during your busiest hour, is there a human to call — or just a help centre?

Non-Negotiables

Three rules that keep this safe

  • 1 Nothing reaches a customer unread. Drafts, always. The moment you let it send unsupervised is the moment it invents a refund policy.
  • 2 Customer data stays out of free tiers. Names, addresses, card details, health or legal specifics. Redact before you paste.
  • 3 Never let it quote a price or a date. Those come from you. It can draft everything around them.
Recap & Hands-On Lab

What to do before next time

The four takeaways

  • Source-of-truth doc before any tool
  • Repetitive, low-judgement, text-shaped
  • Chain the steps, do not cram them
  • Drafts out, never sends out

Your lab

  • Write the source-of-truth doc
  • Find your 10 most-repeated emails
  • Draft replies to three of them
  • Time yourself — bring the number back
