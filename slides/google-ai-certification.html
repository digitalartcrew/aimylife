Before Anything Else

Check the official guide yourself

Google changes exam formats, prerequisites, pricing, and even certificate names. This cohort is the study structure — it is not the exam authority.

Confirm these yourself, today

  • Which certificate you are actually sitting, and its current name
  • Format, length, and how it is scored
  • Cost, and whether any discount or scholarship applies to you
  • Prerequisites, recommended experience, and how long results take

How The Hour Works

Read before. Drill during. Teach after.

Before

Do the module on your own. Arrive with the two things that did not make sense — written down.

During

We drill, not lecture. Practice questions, out loud, with the reasoning exposed.

After

Explain one concept to someone who was not there. If you cannot, you have not learnt it.

Session 2 • Fundamentals Drill

Vocabulary you must own

Not recognise — own. You should be able to define each of these to a friend without notes.

How the model works

  • Token — the unit of text it actually reads
  • Context window — how much it can hold at once
  • Training vs inference — learning vs answering
  • Hallucination — confidently wrong, not lying

How it gets used

  • Grounding — tying answers to real sources
  • RAG — retrieve first, then generate
  • Fine-tuning — adapting a model to a domain
  • Agent — a model that takes actions, not just text
Do Not Skip This

Responsible AI is not the soft section

It is reliably tested, and it is where under-prepared candidates lose marks because they skimmed it.

Fairness & bias

Where bias enters — the data, the labels, the people who chose both — and what mitigation actually looks like.

Privacy & data

What goes into a prompt, where it is stored, and what you are permitted to put there.

Transparency

Telling people when AI was involved, and being able to explain how a result was reached.

Human oversight

Who reviews, who signs off, who is accountable when it is wrong. Always a person.

Practice Technique

Do not memorise the answers

The exam rewords everything. Memorised answers transfer badly; understood ones transfer fine.

  • 1 Answer before you look. Commit out loud, then check. Guessing-then-reading teaches you almost nothing.
  • 2 Explain the wrong ones. For each distractor, say why it is wrong. That is where the understanding lives.
  • 3 Keep an error log. Every miss, with one line on why. Re-read it the morning of the exam.
  • 4 Time one full run. Once, under real conditions, before exam day. Pacing is a skill of its own.
The Capstone

Small, real, and finished

A certificate says you passed. A project says you can do it. The second one is what gets discussed in an interview.

What makes a good one

  • A real problem, ideally one you have
  • Finished and usable, not a prototype
  • You can explain every decision in it
  • Small enough to actually complete

What sinks one

  • Too ambitious to ever finish
  • A tutorial with the names changed
  • No way to show it to anyone
  • Started in the final week
